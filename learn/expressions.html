Numbers and expressions

In the quickstart, one line did all the work: if eq mod i 4 1: tr.add i. This study teaches you to read and write that kind of line: arithmetic, comparisons and logic. On the way, you build off-beat hats and accents from nothing but numbers.

Prefix notation

OType writes the operation first, then its values. There are no parentheses and no precedence to remember: each operation knows how many values it takes, and takes the ones that follow it.

add 1 mul 2 3

add  needs two values:
  1          the first
  mul 2 3    the second, itself an operation: 2 × 3 = 6
= 1 + 6 = 7

To see it for yourself, store the result in a variable and show it with msg, which writes a short text in the box at the end of the run (the simulator prints it too):

a add 1 mul 2 3 is an assignment: a variable name, then a value. Variables have their own study; for now, a is a box that holds a number.

Read a long expression the same way: find the first operation, give it its values one by one, and when a value is itself an operation, complete that one first. eq mod i 4 1 is eq of two values: mod i 4 and 1. So it asks “is the remainder of i by 4 equal to 1?”.

Numbers

Every value is a whole number from −32768 to 32767. There are no fractions: a division drops them, so div 7 2 is 3 and div -7 2 is −3. A result beyond the range stops at its edge (mul 300 300 is 32767) instead of wrapping around, and the arithmetic never fails: dividing by zero gives 0.

writeormeans
add x y+ x yx + y
sub x y- x yx − y
mul x y* x yx × y
div x y/ x yx ÷ y, without the fraction (0 if y is 0)
mod x y% x ythe remainder, never negative: mod -1 4 is 3
min x y · max x ythe smaller · the larger
abs x · neg xwithout its sign · with the opposite sign
lim x lo hix, kept between lo and hi
wrap x lo hix, wrapped around into lo … hi

The symbols are only another spelling: + 1 * 2 3 is the same line as add 1 mul 2 3. A minus sign glued to a number, -3, is a negative number; spaced, - 3 1, it is a subtraction.

True and false

OType has no separate true and false: 0 is false, any other number is true. A comparison gives 1 when it holds and 0 when it does not.

writeoris 1 when
eq x y== x yx equals y
ne x y!= x yx differs from y
lt x y · gt x y< · >x is less · greater than y
le x y · ge x y<= · >=x is at most · at least y
ez x · nz xx is zero · x is not zero

Comparisons combine with logic: and x y, or x y, xor x y (one of the two, not both) and not x. Both sides of and and or are always computed.

Off-beat hats are the odd steps that are not on a beat: an odd step, and not a beat.

Read the condition from the left: and takes two values, eq mod i 2 1 (i is odd) and ne mod i 4 1 (i is not 1, 5, 9 or 13). Steps 3, 7, 11 and 15 remain.

Choosing a value

? c a b is worth a when c is true, otherwise b. Only the chosen side is computed. Here it gives each step a volume: loud on the beats, soft in between.

Two new things here. The line l 1 16: ends with its :, so the loop repeats the indented lines below it, a block, instead of the rest of the line. And pl.set s param value writes a parameter lock on a step: amp.vol is the volume of the AMP page.

Rhythms in one word

Some operations are whole rhythms. er k n s is 1 when step s plays in a Euclidean rhythm of k hits spread over n steps:

dr.v c s gives a velocity from one of eight ready-made curves, step by step; you will see it in the tracks and steps study.

Try it

  • Compute sub 10 mul 2 add 1 2 in your head, then check it with msg.
  • Write the snare of a backbeat: a trig on steps 5 and 13 only, in one if.
  • Make the accents grow through the bar: a volume of add 40 mul i 5.
